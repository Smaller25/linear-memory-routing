# 0005 — SSC routing: the first net win over vanilla at 8k (2026-06-17)

GRM (0003/0004) only generalized to ~2× its trained segment count — it sums gated contributions
over ALL cached checkpoints, so interference grows with segment count and it collapsed at 8192 (32
segments). SSC routes each token to a **fixed top-k of cached checkpoints**, so the read-out fan-in
is constant regardless of how many segments exist — predicted to generalize far better. Tested by
training SSC at the *same* setting as GRM-2048 and evaluating at 8k.

## Setup
`scripts/train_grm_passkey.py --variant ssc --topk 2 --train-len 2048 --batch 2 --steps 250`.
Backbone frozen; only the per-layer router (`nn.Linear(hidden, num_heads*state_size)`, 805M) +
Switch-style load-balance aux trained. Held-out natural-language passkey, chunk 256, memory-light
scorer. train-acc 1.0 throughout.

## Result (held-out passkey)
| length | vanilla | +RM | +GRM @2048 (0004) | **+SSC @2048** | Δ(SSC−vanilla) | segments |
|--------|--------|-----|-------------------|----------------|----------------|----------|
| 512  | 0.993 | 0.993 | 1.000 | 0.993 | +0.000 | 2 |
| 2048 | 0.993 | 0.841 | 0.986 | 0.978 | −0.014 | 8 |
| 4096 | 1.000 | 0.209 | 0.712 | **0.971** | −0.029 | 16 |
| 8192 | 0.738 | 0.000 | 0.000 | **0.986** | **+0.248** | 32 |

## Findings
- **First net win over vanilla.** At 8192, vanilla's fixed state degrades to 0.738; **SSC reaches
  0.986 (+0.248)** — the cached-memory routing lets a *frozen* mamba2 recall where its single state
  fails. This is the project's core thesis, demonstrated.
- **SSC generalizes across segment counts; GRM does not.** Both trained at 8 segments: at 32
  segments (8192) SSC holds (0.986) while GRM collapses (0.000); at 16 segments (4096) SSC 0.971 vs
  GRM 0.712. The constant top-k fan-in (no interference growth) is the mechanism, as hypothesized.
- SSC ≈ vanilla at short lengths (no regression where vanilla already solves the task) and pulls
  ahead exactly where the fixed state runs out — the desired shape.

## Caveats / next
- The load-balance aux (~6.3, large) pushes the router toward uniform checkpoint usage, which is in
  tension with concentrating on the needle segment; it did not block learning here (train-acc 1.0,
  8k win), but **lowering `aux_scale`** may sharpen routing further — worth an ablation.
- Router is 805M params (full `hidden→num_heads*state_size`); a **low-rank router** should keep the
  win far cheaper.
- Push further: eval **16384** (vanilla collapses harder there) for a larger margin; sweep topk;
  test on a higher-load recall task (MQAR with more pairs) where vanilla fails earlier.
