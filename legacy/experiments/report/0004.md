# 0004 — Longer-context GRM + memory-light long eval (2026-06-15)

Follow-up to 0003. Two changes: (1) fixed the eval-memory OOM so we can score 8k–16k; (2) retrained
the GRM gate at a longer context (8 segments) to test the length-generalization gap seen at 4096.

## Memory-light eval (OOM fix)
`run_segmented_lm(return_hidden=True)` returns post-`norm_f` hidden `[b,L,H]`; `eval_recall.
score_hidden` applies `lm_head` only at labelled positions — no full-vocab logits over every
segment. Unblocks 8k–16k. (`scripts/eval_long.py`, and `train_grm_passkey`'s built-in eval.)

## Retrain: GRM at train_len 2048 (8 segments), batch 2, 250 steps
Held-out passkey, chunk 256, memory-light scorer:

| length | vanilla | +RM | +GRM @train1024 (0003) | **+GRM @train2048** | segments |
|--------|--------|-----|------------------------|---------------------|----------|
| 512  | 0.993 | 0.993 | 1.000 | **1.000** | 2 |
| 2048 | 0.993 | 0.841 | 0.993 | **0.986** | 8 |
| 4096 | 1.000 | 0.209 | 0.522 | **0.712** | 16 |
| 8192 | 0.738 | 0.000 | 0.000 | **0.000** | 32 |

## Findings (honest)
- **Training at more segments improves the nearby-longer regime**: 4096 (16 seg) rose 0.522 → 0.712
  when the gate was trained at 8 instead of 4 segments. The gate genuinely learns segment routing.
- **But generalization reaches only ~2× the trained segment count, then collapses**: trained@4seg
  held to ~8 seg; trained@8seg holds to ~16 seg (4096=0.712) but still dies at 32 seg (8192=0.000).
- **Vanilla also degrades at 8192 (0.738)** — headroom for a net MC win exists there, but GRM cannot
  capture it until it generalizes to that segment count.
- **Still no net win over vanilla.** GRM matches vanilla only where vanilla already ~solves the task.

## Why, and what next
GRM sums **gated contributions over ALL cached checkpoints**, so interference grows with the number
of segments — a model trained at N segments has not seen the interference pattern of 4N. Two levers:
1. **SSC (top-k routing)** should generalize across segment counts far better than GRM: it selects a
   *fixed* top-k of cached states regardless of how many exist, so the readout's effective fan-in is
   constant. This is the most promising next experiment.
2. **Curriculum / mixed-length training** (sample train_len ∈ {1024…8192} per step) so the gate sees
   many segment counts.
Then evaluate at 8k–16k where vanilla itself collapses, to test for a *net* MC gain (the real goal).
