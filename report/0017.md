# 0017 — Supervised method on Selective Copying: read-out wins, boundaries recovered, but adaptivity needs a budget (2026-06-28)

0015 showed (on our in-house irregular MQAR) that a segment-cache read-out with **oracle / learned**
boundaries beats a fixed stride. The open ask: **validate that supervised method on a recognized
external benchmark**, not a bespoke generator. This report runs **Selective Copying** (Gu & Dao 2023,
`lmr/tasks/selective_copying.py`) — M data tokens at random positions among `8M` noise tokens,
reproduced in order after a marker — the standard content-vs-position task. Same backbone/recipe as
0015 (GDN-2 d256/4L/4heads, ~2M params at vocab 256, train M∈{4..64}, 8000 steps).

## Result (recall acc vs M = #data tokens to copy)
| Selective Copying | M16 | M32 | M64 | **M128** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| vanilla (GDN-2) | 1.000 | 0.998 | 0.989 | **0.592** |
| mosc **fixed chunk=2** | 0.999 | 0.998 | 0.997 | **0.994** |
| mosc **oracle** | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.999 | **0.998** |
| mosc **learned** (oracle-distilled) | 1.000 | 0.999 | 0.999 | **0.996** |

Learned-boundary quality (jobs 1285–1288): **precision = recall = top-k(p)∩data = 1.00 at every M**,
pred/seq exactly = #data (16/32/64/128); threshold sweep flat at 1.00 for cutoffs 0.5→0.05.

## Two findings

**1. The supervised method transfers — cleanly.** The segment-cache read-out turns vanilla's collapse
at M128 (**0.592**) into **~1.0** (fixed/oracle/learned all ≥0.994). The learned head **perfectly
recovers the data positions** (prec/rec/top-k all 1.00, exactly #data boundaries) — even cleaner than
on irregular MQAR (0015) — and its segment lengths are a **genuine variable distribution** (median 6,
mean 8.9, max 164 @M128) that tracks the 1/8 data density, not a fixed period. So "boundaries are
learnable and content-adaptive" (0015) reproduces on a standard task: the head finds content at
irregular positions with no fixed-stride assumption.

**2. But with an unconstrained cache, Selective Copying does NOT require adaptivity.** Unlike irregular
MQAR (0015: fixed chunk=2 → **0.00**), here **fixed chunk=2 also hits ~0.99**. Reason: every data token
is a needle and the value *is* the token itself, so chunk=2 over-segments the stream into ~T/2 ≫ #data
slots and the ample cache captures every needle — wasting slots on noise costs nothing when the cache
is free. This **reaffirms the 0016 lesson**: these recall tasks discriminate *adaptive vs fixed*
boundaries only under a **tight cache budget**. Selective Copying with a full cache discriminates only
*read-out vs vanilla* (a real win), not *where you cut*.

## Verdict & implication
- **Validated:** (a) the segment-cache read-out gives a clean net win over the saturating vanilla on a
  standard benchmark (0.59→0.99 @M128); (b) the supervised learned head recovers the irregular content
  positions **perfectly** (1.00) with adaptive segment lengths — the 0015 claim holds off our own task.
- **Scoped honestly:** the *adaptivity necessity* (oracle/learned **beating** fixed) does **not** show
  here, because an unconstrained cache makes over-segmentation free. To surface it, Selective Copying
  must be run **under a cache budget** — exactly axis-2 (`--cache-mode capped|hier --cache-budget B`):
  with tight B, fixed chunk=2's noise segments crowd out data while oracle/density boundaries spend the
  budget only on needles. That comparison is **0019** (axis-2 also runs on this task).
