# 0003 — Phase 1: training the GRM gate recovers what RM destroyed (2026-06-15)

Phase-0b showed training-free RM collapses a frozen mamba2's recall (it sums every cached
checkpoint blindly: passkey@4096 1.000 → 0.209). Phase 1 trains **only the per-layer GRM gate**
(`W_u`; the 1.3B backbone is frozen) on the in-distribution natural-language passkey, then evals
vanilla vs +RM vs +trained-GRM on held-out passkeys.

## Setup
- `lmr/scripts/train_grm_passkey.py`: backbone frozen, GRM heads only. Train on **multi-segment**
  passkey (train_len 1024 = 4 segments @ chunk 256) so the cache is non-empty and the gate gets
  gradient. batch 4, 200 steps, AdamW lr 1e-3, fp32, A100. Fresh keys/depths each step.
- Fix: `mamba_chunk_scan_combined` backward asserts `D.stride(-1)==1` → make `D` contiguous in
  `segment_runner._run_scan` (training/backward path).
- Trainable head params **805M** (`W_u`: 2048×8192 per layer × 48; descriptor_dim = 64 heads × 128
  state). Large — a low-rank `W_u` is an obvious future reduction. Train loss 1.01 → ~0.02.

## Result (held-out passkey, chunk 256)
| length | vanilla | +RM | **+GRM (trained)** | Δ(GRM−vanilla) | segments |
|--------|--------|-----|--------------------|----------------|----------|
| 512  | 0.993 | 0.993 | **1.000** | +0.007 | 2 |
| 1024 | 0.986 | 0.965 | **1.000** | +0.014 | 4 |
| 2048 | 0.993 | 0.841 | **0.993** | +0.000 | 8 |
| 4096 | 1.000 | 0.209 | **0.482** | −0.518 | 16 |

## Conclusion (honest)
- **Training the gate works.** GRM ≥ RM at every length, by a large margin where RM had collapsed:
  2048 (RM 0.841 → GRM 0.993, full vanilla recovery), 4096 (RM 0.209 → GRM 0.482, >2×). This
  directly answers the open question from 0002: the frozen-model failure of MC is **fixable by
  training a small routing head** — no backbone retrain.
- **Two honest limits:**
  1. **No net win over vanilla yet.** In the trained regime (≤2048) GRM only *matches* vanilla
     (~1.0). The passkey is easy enough that the fixed-state vanilla already scores ~100%, so there
     is **no headroom** for MC to demonstrate its capacity advantage. To show MC *exceeding* a fixed
     state we need a regime where **vanilla itself fails** (longer context where mamba2 collapses,
     or higher-load MQAR) — and the eval-memory OOM at ≥8k must be fixed first (score labelled
     positions only, not full-vocab logits).
  2. **Length generalization gap.** GRM was trained at 4 segments (1024); at 16 segments (4096) it
     recovers only partially (0.482). Training at longer context (8–16 segments) should close this.

## Next

- Re-train GRM at longer context; eval where **vanilla collapses** (8k–16k) to test for a *net* MC win.
