# 0008 — Mechanism comparison: only hard top-k (SSC) generalizes to long context (2026-06-17)

Head-to-head of all read-out mechanisms on the proven testbed (frozen mamba2-1.3b, where vanilla
passkey collapses 1.000→0.738 by 8k). All trained heads: backbone frozen, **low-rank router (r=64)**,
trained on the natural-language passkey at train_len 2048 (8 segments), chunk 256. Held-out eval
(8192 via fast n=16; others n=64), memory-light scorer.

| length | vanilla | +RM (no train) | +GRM | +SSC | +AoM | +MoM | segments |
|--------|--------|----------------|------|------|------|------|----------|
| 512  | 0.993 | 0.993 | 1.000 | 0.993 | 0.993 | 0.993 | 2 |
| 2048 | 0.993 | 0.841 | 0.986 | 0.978 | 0.935 | 0.986 | 8 |
| 4096 | 1.000 | 0.209 | 0.712 | **0.971** | 0.655 | 0.820 | 16 |
| 8192 | 0.738 | 0.000 | 0.000 | **0.986** | 0.014 | 0.053 | 32 |

## The result
- **Only SSC (hard top-k routing) generalizes to long context.** At 8192 (32 segments) SSC holds
  0.986 (+0.248 over vanilla) while **every other mechanism collapses**: AoM 0.014, MoM 0.053,
  GRM 0.000, RM 0.000. The ordering is consistent and sharpens with length: at 4096 SSC 0.971 > MoM
  0.820 > GRM 0.712 > AoM 0.655 > RM 0.209.
- **Why hard sparsity wins.** All were trained at 8 segments and tested at up to 32. The failure mode
  is *dilution by distractors*:
  - **SSC** hard-selects top-k=2 of the N cached checkpoints → the read-out sums ~2 contributions
    (the needle's + one), clean regardless of N.
  - **AoM** (dense softmax over all N) spreads weight across 32 checkpoints; even normalized, the
    weighted sum drowns the needle among 31 distractor contributions → collapses (0.014).
  - **MoM** merges all N checkpoints into M=4 slots; blending 32 states into 4 slots mixes the needle
    with distractors → dilutes (0.053).
  - **GRM** sums all N gated contributions → interference grows with N → 0.000 (cf. 0004).
  So the key property is **sparse hard selection**, not just "routing" or "normalization" — dense or
  merge-based combination loses the needle as the segment count grows.

## Takeaways
- SSC is the mechanism to carry forward. AoM/MoM, despite the MoM-paper pedigree and AoM's
  softmax-normalization, do **not** generalize across segment counts here — a clean negative result.
- Practical note: the in-script 8192 eval (n=64, segmented) is very slow; use the fast n=16 path
  (`score_hidden`, micro_batch 1) for long-length numbers — same conclusion, minutes not hours.
## Hierarchical caching does NOT rescue the dense methods
Tested the hypothesis that bounding the checkpoint count (merge every K=4 segments into one coarse
checkpoint → 32 segments become ~8 checkpoints) would cut distractors and rescue AoM. Trained
AoM + `--hierarchical-k 4` (low-rank 64, train_len 2048):

| length | flat AoM | AoM + hier-k4 |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| 2048 | 0.935 | 0.986 |
| 4096 | 0.655 | 0.489 |
| 8192 | 0.014 | 0.105 |

Mixed and still collapsing: 8192 nudges 0.014→0.105 but stays far below SSC's 0.986; 4096 actually
*regresses*. **Merging doesn't help** because a coarse checkpoint (sum of K segment states) blends
the needle's segment with distractors — the same dilution that sinks MoM's slot-merge. The needle
must be kept *separable*, which only hard top-k selection (SSC) achieves.

## Final verdict
**SSC (hard top-k routing) is the mechanism.** Dense softmax (AoM), slot-merge (MoM), gated-sum
(GRM), and coarse hierarchical merging all dilute the needle as the segment count grows past training
and collapse at 8k; only SSC's sparse hard selection generalizes (0.986, +0.248 over vanilla). Carry
SSC forward (it also scaled to 2.7b, +0.50 — report 0007).
