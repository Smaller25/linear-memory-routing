# 0009 — SSC top-k sweep + RULER validation (2026-06-17)

Two questions: (a) how sparse must SSC's routing be? (b) does the result hold on a *standard*
benchmark (NVIDIA RULER), not just our self-made passkey?

## A. SSC top-k sweep (mamba2-1.3b, frozen; low-rank-64 router trained @train_len 2048)
Held-out natural-language passkey (8192 via fast n=16):

| top-k | 2048 | 4096 | 8192 |
|-------|------|------|------|
| k=1 | 0.080 | 0.050 | 0.053 |
| k=2 | 0.978 | 0.971 | 0.986 |
| k=4 | 0.978 | 0.978 | **1.000** |
| k=8 | 0.978 | 0.971 | **1.000** |

- **k=1 collapses** — top-1 is too brittle: the router must pick the single needle segment exactly,
  and any miss gives zero signal (no redundancy).
- **k≥2 all strong; k=4/8 reach 1.0 at 8192.** Larger k does *not* degrade (cf. the worry that it
  would approach AoM's dense failure). The key is **hard selection of a bounded subset (k ≪ N
  segments)** — at 8192 (32 segments) even k=8 selects 8 and the trained router keeps the needle in
  the top-k. AoM (dense softmax over *all* 32) dilutes and collapses (0.014); SSC does not. So it's
  *sparse hard selection*, not *extreme* sparsity, that matters. Sweet spot k ∈ [2, 8].

## B. RULER validation (standard synthetic long-context; real Paul-Graham haystack)
Vendored NVIDIA RULER (`src/ruler`, `scripts/ruler.py prepare`); single-answer tasks scored with the
same teacher-forced scorer. **SSC = the k=4 head trained on our passkey — evaluated ZERO-SHOT on
RULER** (no RULER-specific training):

| task | len | vanilla | +RM | +SSC (zero-shot) |
|------|-----|--------|-----|------------------|
| niah_single_1 | 4096 | 0.706 | 0.485 | **0.890** |
| niah_single_1 | 8192 | 0.753 | 0.000 | **0.815** |
| niah_multikey_2 | 4096 | 0.115 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| niah_multikey_2 | 8192 | ~0 | ~0 | ~0 |

- **niah_single_1: SSC transfers zero-shot and beats vanilla** (+0.184 @4096, +0.062 @8192). The
  router learned on our passkey generalizes to RULER's differently-worded single-needle task
  ("What is the special magic number for capable-radiosonde…") — validation on the **standard
  benchmark**, not just our own task. (training-free RM collapses, as everywhere.)
- **niah_multikey_2: both near-floor.** mamba2-1.3b vanilla is itself weak on multi-key (0.115),
  and the passkey-trained SSC does NOT transfer to multi-key disambiguation — it learned
  single-needle routing. Honest limitation: multi-key needs SSC trained on a multi-key task (our
  `make_text_multikey` or RULER niah_multikey as the training distribution).

## Takeaways
- **k ∈ [2,8] hard top-k is robust; k=1 too brittle.** Carry k=2–4.
- **SSC validates on standard RULER niah_single (zero-shot, +0.06–0.18 over vanilla)** — the result
  is not an artifact of our self-made passkey.
- **Open:** train SSC on a multi-key distribution and re-check RULER niah_multikey; full RULER
  free-generation metric (vs our teacher-forced proxy) for publication-grade numbers.
