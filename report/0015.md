# 0015 — Adaptive boundaries: irregular MQAR shows fixed-stride fails, learned ≈ oracle (2026-06-22)

0013 found the learned head segments "per fact (length 2)". But standard MQAR lays facts at a **fixed
period** `[k,v,k,v,…]`, so a length-2 segment is just a fixed stride — the result could not tell
"learned to find facts" from "learned a period-2 chunk". This report settles it: on an **irregular**
layout a fixed stride **fails** while the learned head still tracks the facts.

## The degeneracy, confirmed
Fixed `chunk=2` on standard MQAR equals the oracle (job 1247): **1.00 / 1.00 / 1.00 / 0.999** at
kv 64/128/256/512 — identical to oracle (0012). So on regular MQAR, adaptivity is neither needed nor
tested.

## Irregular MQAR (`make_mqar_gapped`)
The `(k,v)` units are separated by random-length filler (`--ctx-filler 128`), so facts sit at
content-dependent, non-periodic positions; `value_pos` (per row) is the oracle. From-scratch GDN-2
(d256/4L), train-kv 4–128 (vanilla 4–32), eval 64–512.

| model | kv64 | kv128 | kv256 | kv512 |
|-------|-----|------|------|------|
| vanilla GDN-2 (saturates)      | 0.82 | 0.30 | 0.06 | 0.01 |
| mosc **fixed chunk=2**         | **0.00** | **0.00** | **0.00** | **0.00** |
| mosc oracle (true value pos)   | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.97 |
| mosc **learned** boundaries    | ~1.0 | ~1.0 | ~1.0 | **1.00** |

Learned-boundary quality on irregular: **precision = recall = 1.00 at every kv** (predicted count ≈
kv) — the head places boundaries exactly at the random value positions.

**Verdict.** A fixed stride collapses on irregular facts (0.00) while the learned head matches the
oracle (~1.0) by adapting boundaries to where the facts actually are. Adaptive boundaries are both
**necessary** (fixed fails) and **learned** (precision/recall 1.0). The 0013 "length-2" picture was
the degenerate projection onto a fixed-period task.

## Segment-length distribution: degenerate vs adaptive
| layout | kv64 | kv128 | kv256 | kv512 | shape |
|--------|------|-------|-------|-------|-------|
| regular (0013)   | all **2** | all 2 | all 2 | all 2 | a spike (fixed period) |
| **irregular**    | median **4** (2–11) | 3 (2–9) | 2 (2–7) | 2 (2–6) | a **distribution** tracking the random gaps |

On irregular the segment length = inter-fact distance (filler gap + the 2-token fact), so it is a real
distribution; as kv rises against the fixed filler budget the gaps shrink and lengths fall toward 2.
The learned segmentation follows the content, not a clock. (Figure: artifact `adaptive-boundaries`.)

## Bugs fixed en route (don't re-discover)
1. **Context filler must be blank, not random** — randomising it injects fake keys/values into the
   context and destroys the associations (every method, incl. oracle, scored exactly 0.000).
2. **Segment summary = boundary-token hidden, not mean-pool** — mean-pooling a segment dilutes the
   fact across the filler, so oracle collapsed to 0.000 on irregular while vanilla learned; using the
   hidden at the segment-end (value) token recovers it (oracle 0.000 → ~1.0). The 0012/0013 mean-pool
   only worked because regular segments were exactly the 2-token fact.

## Caveats
Boundaries are still learned WITH oracle distillation (unsupervised remains open); the summary is the
boundary-hidden proxy (true per-row recurrent-state read-out not yet implemented); synthetic + small.
