# 0024 — Why MC-SSC(mean-pool) GDN2-370M fails multi-NIAH: routing is the dominant bottleneck (2026-07-26)

An end-to-end-trained Memory-Caching (mean-pool descriptor) SSC on GDN-2 370M does **single**-NIAH well
but collapses on **multi-key** NIAH. This report decomposes that failure along the causal chain
**write → read → route → generation** with three measured experiments (E1 routing accuracy, E2 oracle-
routing intervention, E3 per-layer write/read fidelity) and fills in the design spec's decision table
(`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-25-mc-niah-analysis-design.md` §4).

**TL;DR.** Write fidelity is **intact** (no single-vs-multi write-side collapse anywhere). Routing is
**broken**, worst when the distractor key sits in a *different* segment than gold (D): hit@2 ≈0.50–0.56
vs chance 0.286, and among key-bearing segments the router picks the *wrong* key's segment more often
than a coin flip. **Oracle injection of the gold chunk into top-k raises accuracy in all 4 (model ×
S/D) cells** — mc-30B/D 0.062→0.562 — which is direct causal evidence that routing, not the state, is
the dominant bottleneck. Read-side is *partially* broken and only under key collision (S): the
answer-position readout drifts from its single-needle twin to cos ≈0.90–0.93 at late layers, while D
stays ≥0.98. 30B improves routing and the anchor score (MK-NIAH-1 2→32) but oracle still recovers a lot
on top of it → **routing remains the main lever**; the residual post-oracle gap is read-side + gate/
generation. Verdict = spec row 3 (**routing failure**) with a row-2 admixture confined to condition S.

## 2. E1 — routing accuracy (does the router find the gold chunk?)

SSC routing scores recomputed at the answer position for all 16 MC layers
(`u = connector(h)`, `score_i = <u, mean-pool summary_i>`), past segments only; samples whose gold
needle falls in the *current* segment are structurally un-routable and excluded (`eligible`;
5/50 single, 3/50 multikey, 0/16 both paired). Figure: `0024_figs/e1_routing.png` (hit@2 vs layer,
one panel per model, one line per dataset).

**Chance is dataset-specific and was measured**, not assumed (`mean(min(1, 2/cur_seg))`):

| dataset | mean past segments | chance hit@2 |
|---|---|---|
| niah_single_1 | 6.96 | 0.288 |
| niah_multikey_1 | 4.19 | **0.486** |
| paired_S_multi / paired_D_multi | 7.00 | 0.286 |

RULER's multikey template puts the query earlier relative to the needles, so its contexts are
*shorter* at the answer position and its chance level is nearly double the others — raw hit@2 is not
comparable across datasets without this.

**hit@2 at each dataset's best layer** (eligible samples only; `gold_rank_mean` = mean rank of gold in
the masked score vector, 0 = top-1):

| model | niah_single_1 | niah_multikey_1 | paired_S_multi | paired_D_multi |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mc-5B | **0.933** (L14, rank 0.44) | 0.660 (L2, rank 1.06) | 0.812 (L14, rank 0.69) | **0.562** (L14, rank 2.19) |
| mc-30B | **1.000** (L15, rank 0.20) | 0.809 (L14, rank 0.91) | 0.750 (L14, rank 1.00) | **0.500** (L0, rank 2.00) |

1. **Single-needle routing is essentially solved; multi-key is not.** Single reaches 0.93/1.00 against
   chance 0.288 (3.2–3.5× chance). Multikey's 0.66/0.81 is against chance **0.486** — only 1.36×/1.66×.
2. **Different-segment distractors (D) are the hard case.** paired_D best-layer hit@2 = 0.562 (5B) /
   0.500 (30B) vs paired_S 0.812 / 0.750; gold's mean rank in D is ≈2 (i.e. typically *outside* top-2)
   vs ≈0.7–1.0 in S. mc-30B's paired_D best layer is layer **0** (0.50) and its late layers sit at
   0.25 — at/below chance — so no depth carries a usable D signal for that checkpoint.
3. **The router discriminates "key-bearing vs not" far better than "which key".** `top1_amongkeys`
   (is the queried key's segment ranked first *among* key-bearing past segments; chance 0.5) is
   ≈0.53 (mc-5B) / 0.47 (mc-30B) on `niah_multikey_1` — a coin flip, flat across layers. On Dataset B
   at the best layer it splits by condition: S 0.69/0.75 but **D 0.19 (3/16) / 0.375 (6/16)** — in D the
   router prefers the *distractor's* segment over gold, below chance (n=16, so directional).
4. **Layer profiles differ in kind, not just magnitude.** Single is near-chance through layers 1–13
   then jumps at L14–15 (0.91–1.00) — routing information is concentrated in the last 1–2 layers.
   Multikey is flatter: already ≈0.60–0.64 from layer 2 and no late takeoff. (Note for layers 1–13
   multikey is *higher* than single in raw hit@2, which reverses once each dataset's own chance level is
   accounted for.) Paired curves are noisy mid-stack (0.19–0.44) with a single L14 spike.

Sanity gate (single hit@2 clearly above chance and not flat-at-chance everywhere, given 30B scores 92
on S-NIAH-1) passes → no routing/eligibility bug suspected.

## 3. E2 — oracle routing injection (the causal main experiment)

`GDN2SSC` subclassed so that, at every layer, the gold segment is forced into the top-k whenever it is
eligible but not already selected, with injected score `max(selected_max, online_score)` so the gate
cannot structurally down-weight it. Dataset B multi rows, `n_gen=128` greedy, per condition per model
(n=16/cell). Verified no-op when inactive: with `gold_segment=None` the patched model produces
**byte-identical** generations to a stock `GDN2SSC` (checked on all 4 model×condition calls).

| model | condition | baseline | oracle | Δ | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mc-5B | S | 0.125 | 0.312 | **+0.188** | 16 |
| mc-5B | D | 0.188 | 0.500 | **+0.312** | 16 |
| mc-30B | S | 0.000 | 0.312 | **+0.312** | 16 |
| mc-30B | D | 0.062 | **0.562** | **+0.500** | 16 |

1. **Oracle > baseline in all 4 cells**, by 3–8 samples of 16. Repairing routing *alone* — weights,
   gate softmax and online path untouched — is sufficient to move a large block of failures to correct.
   This upgrades E1's routing gap from correlational to **causal**.
2. **The intervention helps most exactly where E1 said routing is worst: D.** Δ(D) > Δ(S) for both
   models, and the single biggest jump (mc-30B/D, 0.062→0.562) is the cell whose baseline routing was
   worst (best-layer hit@2 = 0.500 at layer 0).
3. **30B does not remove the need.** mc-30B routes better (E1) and scores 32 vs 2 on the MK-NIAH-1
   anchor, yet oracle injection still adds +0.31/+0.50 — more headroom than mc-5B, not less. Scaling
   tokens improves the router but does not make routing stop being the binding constraint.
4. **Oracle does not saturate** (0.31–0.56, not ~1.0): ~44–69% of pairs stay wrong with perfect
   routing. That residual is the read-side + gate/generation part of the chain (§4), and it is why the
   verdict is not "routing only".

Baseline scores are much lower than E1's hit@2 rates because `correct` additionally requires the full
128-token greedy generation to surface the 7-digit number in the right form; the controlled comparison
is oracle-vs-baseline within a cell, which is unaffected. The routing-hit × baseline-correctness
cross-table (`e1_routing.json:e2_join`, model-separated) shows no positive hit→correct relation
(mc-5B paired_D: hit 0/9 vs miss 3/7; mc-30B ~all-zero) — at n=7–13 per bucket with a near-zero
baseline this table carries almost no information and is **not** part of the causal claim.

## 4. E3 — per-layer write/read fidelity (is it a state/decoding failure?)

Prompt-only single forward, no generation. Per (model, condition, variant, layer), the memory state is
rebuilt with training's exact `chunk_gdn2` call and the linear readout `r = q·M` is decomposed:
`b1_after` = cos(k̂_t*·M_prefix, v_t*) with the scan stopped right after the value token (write
upper bound), `b1_final` = same against the *stored* segment-final state, `b2_qk_align` =
cos(q_Ta, k_t*), `b2_interf_ratio` = ‖r − (q·k)v‖/‖(q·k)v‖, `b2_read_cos_vs_single` = cos of the
multi row's readout against its token-aligned single twin. Figure: `0024_figs/e3_fidelity.png`.
Axis order of the kernel's `[H,K,V]` state was verified both from source and empirically
(reconstruction cos 0.77/0.93 for the correct contraction vs ≈−0.01 for the transpose).

Layer-mean (16 layers, 16 pairs):

| model | cond | variant | b1_after | b1_final | b2_qk_align | b2_interf_ratio | b2_read_cos_vs_single |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mc-5B | S | single | 0.779 | 0.445 | −0.004 | 3.26 | — |
| mc-5B | S | multi | 0.780 | 0.443 | −0.004 | 3.41 | **0.974** |
| mc-5B | D | single | 0.786 | 0.460 | −0.005 | 3.42 | — |
| mc-5B | D | multi | 0.786 | 0.452 | −0.004 | 3.53 | 0.995 |
| mc-30B | S | single | 0.816 | 0.527 | 0.003 | 2.77 | — |
| mc-30B | S | multi | 0.813 | 0.530 | 0.003 | 2.81 | **0.974** |
| mc-30B | D | single | 0.819 | 0.542 | 0.002 | 2.82 | — |
| mc-30B | D | multi | 0.818 | 0.537 | 0.002 | 2.80 | 0.996 |

1. **No write-side collapse, and no single→multi write penalty** anywhere: `b1_after` is 0.68–0.97
   per-layer (≥0.5 on every layer/model/condition) and single vs multi differ by ≤0.01. *Scope caveat:*
   in condition S the single variant carries a **length-matched neutral filler** where the multi variant
   has the distractor, so this compares filler-vs-distractor, not nothing-vs-distractor. The narrow,
   supported claim: gold-token write fidelity is insensitive to *what* the co-resident content is.
2. **Within-segment erosion is real and S/D-independent:** `b1_final` ≪ `b1_after` (e.g. mc-5B 0.78 →
   0.44; per-layer as low as 0.18 at layer 7). Writes that continue *after* the value token measurably
   degrade the stored state's readability of that value by the end of the 256-token segment — an
   architectural (decay/overwrite) cost, not a multi-key effect.
3. **The read side breaks only under key collision (S).** `b2_read_cos_vs_single` decays with depth in S
   to **0.929 (mc-5B) / 0.903 (mc-30B) at layer 15** (dipping to ≈0.93 already at layer 8), while D stays
   **≥0.979 / ≥0.984 at every layer** (≥0.999 for layers 0–7). A same-key distractor sharing the gold
   segment perturbs *what gets read out at the answer position* even though it leaves the gold write
   intact — the S failure is a **read/disambiguation** failure at the state level, distinct in mechanism
   from D's routing failure.
4. **Caveat on the two absolute read diagnostics.** `b2_interf_ratio` > 1 at *every* layer/condition/
   model (1.25–7.6): the single-binding term explains a minority of `r` everywhere. `b2_qk_align` ≈ 0
   (±0.01–0.05): the answer-position query is uncorrelated with the needle key in raw cosine. Both are
   plausible for distributed associative readout but mean these two should not be read as standalone
   write/read health scores — only the *paired* contrast (`b2_read_cos_vs_single`) is a controlled signal.

## 5. Decision table (spec §4), filled with measured evidence

| stage | verdict | measured evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **b-1 write** | **✓ intact** | `b1_after` 0.68–0.97 per layer, ≥0.5 everywhere; single vs multi Δ ≤0.01 in both S and D (E3 §4.1). Separate, S/D-independent within-segment erosion `b1_final` ≪ `b1_after` (0.78→0.44) is an architectural cost, not the multi-key failure. |
| **b-2 read** | **partially ✗ — only in S** | `b2_read_cos_vs_single`: S drifts to 0.929/0.903 by layer 15; D stays ≥0.98 everywhere (E3 §4.3). Key-collision-specific readout perturbation. `qk_align`/`interf_ratio` uninformative as absolutes (§4.4). |
| **E1 route** | **✗ — badly, worst in D** | paired_D hit@2 0.562/0.500 vs chance 0.286 and gold rank ≈2 (outside top-2); `amongkeys` in D 0.19/0.375 (below the 0.5 coin flip); multikey 0.66/0.81 vs its own chance 0.486 (E1 §2). |
| **E2 oracle recovery** | **✓ — in all cells, largest in D** | +0.188/+0.312 (mc-5B S/D), +0.312/+0.500 (mc-30B S/D), oracle > baseline in 4/4 (E2 §3). |

**Verdict.** The measured pattern is spec **row 3 — routing failure (`✓ ✓ ✗ ✓`) — as the dominant
mechanism**, with a row-2 (readout) admixture that is **confined to condition S**, and a residual
row-4 (gate/online-path + generation) component that oracle routing does not repair. Concretely: the
memory *contains* the gold binding (write ✓), but the mean-pool descriptor cannot single out *which*
cached segment holds the queried key when several segments hold keys — it separates key-bearing from
non-key segments, not key from key — and that failure alone accounts for a large block of MK-NIAH
errors, since forcing the gold chunk into top-k lifts every cell (up to 0.062→0.562). Where the
competing key shares the gold segment, routing is not even the right question: the correct segment is
retrieved and the *readout* drifts instead, so no router can fix it. 30B tokens improve the router
(E1) and the anchor (MK-NIAH-1 2→32) but leave the largest oracle headroom of the two checkpoints, so
this is not a deficiency that more pretraining resolves.

**Implication for the memory-routing algorithm (two parts, and they are not interchangeable).**
(a) *Cross-segment key selection (D)* — the higher-yield lever: descriptors that preserve per-key
identity instead of averaging it away (multi-vector / per-key or per-fact descriptors, query-conditioned
scoring, or finer chunking so keys do not share a segment), which is what oracle injection is
simulating. (b) *Within-segment key collision (S)* — needs **read-side** disambiguation at the state
level (per-key slots, a query-conditioned second-stage read inside the selected segment); routing
cannot help here by construction, which caps how far (a) alone can go. This also connects to 0010's
scope statement (segment-level routing cannot disambiguate keys clustered in a segment) — that claim is
now decomposed into a routing part and a readout part, with the routing part shown to be causally
dominant and repairable.

## 6. Limitations (honest)

- **E0 not re-measured.** The anchor table is the collaborator's eval, taken as given per instruction.
  Dataset B absolute scores are *not* comparable to it (own template, 16 pairs, controlled distractors);
  e.g. mc-30B's Dataset B baseline (0.000–0.062) is far below its MK-NIAH-1 anchor of 32.
- **n=16 per E2 cell**, no confidence intervals — the 2×2×2 deltas are directional (though monotone in
  4/4 cells with 3–8-sample margins). Same for `amongkeys` on Dataset B (n=16) and the `e2_join`
  buckets (n=3–13, which is why nothing is concluded from them).
- **Dataset B coverage:** gold and distractor segments are confined to segments **1–5 of 7**, so
  very-early and very-late placements are untested.
- **Chance levels differ across datasets** (`niah_multikey_1` mean 4.19 past segments → chance 0.486 vs
  0.286 elsewhere). Cross-dataset hit@2 comparisons in §2 are always stated against each dataset's own
  chance.
- **bf16 run-to-run noise:** rerunning `routing_stats.py` unchanged shifts per-layer hit@2 by up to
  ~2.3pp at non-peak layers (non-deterministic kernel reduction order). Best-layer conclusions are far
  larger than this; individual mid-stack layer values are not.
- **`e1_routing.json:e2_join` had a model-conflation bug** (a flat `(condition, pair_id)` key let mc-5B
  win every collision); fixed to read `results[model][condition]["baseline"]["rows"]` and re-run. Cite
  **`e2_oracle.json`** for all causal numbers. hit@2/gold_rank/amongkeys were never affected.
- **E1/E3 Dataset-B passes cover the `multi` rows** (E3 additionally the paired `single` rows for the
  twin comparison); there is no Dataset-B single-variant *generation* score, so "fraction of the
  single→multi gap recovered" cannot be computed — only absolute oracle deltas are reported.
- **Not covered:** anti-oracle control (excluded by decision), multiquery/multivalue/multikey_2·3,
  vanilla internals, and any improvement algorithm (this is diagnosis only).
